Ahuramazda and the gods who are

The King's god in the inscriptions, 522-479 BCE

Darius says he is king by the favour of Ahuramazda, and the inscriptions never say otherwise. They also pray to "the other gods who are", a phrase that leaves a door open that the tablets walk straight through.

در بازی: The Persian faith in every level is this: the Sanctuary or Altar of the Magi, the lan and the Sacrifice for the King, and their feasts.

By the favour of Ahuramazda

در اسناد

The Behistun inscription says it over and over: Darius holds the throne because Ahuramazda gave it to him. His tomb inscription at Naqsh-e Rostam opens with a great god who made this earth, that sky, man, and happiness for man, and who made Darius king.

Behistun, DB 5, 9; DNa 1

The other gods

در اسناد

A prayer at Persepolis asks Ahuramazda, with the gods of the royal house, to protect the land from a hostile army, from famine and from the Lie. Nothing in the royal texts of Darius or Xerxes names those other gods. Mithra and Anahita are first named beside him under Artaxerxes II, more than a century after Cyrus.

DPd 3; A2Sa

Xerxes and the daivas

مورد مناقشه

Xerxes says that where the daivas were worshipped he destroyed the place, and commanded that they not be worshipped. He names no place and no people. Scholars have tied it to Babylon, to Egypt, to Greece or to nothing in particular, a king's statement of what kings do. It is the nearest thing to religious war in the royal texts, and we cannot say what it describes.

XPh 4-5; Kuhrt 2007

The Elamites did not worship him

در اسناد

In a fifth column added at Behistun, Darius calls the Elamites faithless and says Ahuramazda was not worshipped by them. Yet his own administration was paying for Elamite gods at the same time, with the same grain. Whatever this is, it isn't a policy of conversion.

Behistun, DB 72, 75; Henkelman 2008
