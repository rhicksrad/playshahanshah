How a Persian sacrificed

Herodotus's picture of the rite

Herodotus gives one close description of a Persian sacrifice, and it is strange on purpose: no altar, no fire, no music, no wine poured out. Some of it the tablets contradict. Some of it they can't test.

در بازی: The Sacrifice for the King follows him: crowds, a priest, a victim, no pipes. It reads the omens, and good ones put heart in your soldiers.

The rite

روایت باستانی

The man who sacrifices leads the victim to a clean place and calls on the god, wearing a myrtle wreath in his tiara. He may not pray for himself alone, only for the King and all Persians, himself among them. He boils the meat, lays it on the softest grass, and a magus sings. Then he takes the meat home.

Herodotus 1.132

White horses and a thousand oxen

روایت باستانی

On the march into Greece, Xerxes sacrifices a thousand oxen to Athena at Ilion while the Magi pour libations to the heroes. At the Strymon the Magi kill white horses for good omens. Off Magnesia they sacrifice to the wind for three days and it stops, or, Herodotus adds, it dropped by itself.

Herodotus 7.43, 7.113-114, 7.191

A Greek diviner for Mardonius

روایت باستانی

Before Plataea, Mardonius used a Greek seer, Hegesistratus of Elis, who had escaped the Spartans by cutting off his own foot. His victims said to wait and defend. Mardonius lost patience, attacked, and died.

Herodotus 9.37-41
