Egypt's temples under Persia

Neith, the Apis, Amun at Hibis

Egyptian documents show Persian kings acting as Pharaoh, paying for temples and burying a sacred bull. Greek stories show Cambyses stabbing it. Both were written by people with reasons.

در بازی: Memphis's main temple is Ptah's. The Persian garrison has its own altar. Its feasts are Sokar's and the new Apis's, and Mourn the Apis is its rite.

Neith's temple at Sais

در اسناد

Udjahorresnet's statue says Cambyses cleared foreigners camped in Neith's temple, gave back its revenues and festivals, and worshipped there himself. He wrote this to show his own worth to his town, and he is the only witness.

Statue of Udjahorresnet (Vatican); Posener 1936

The Apis

مورد مناقشه

An epitaph from the Serapeum shows Cambyses kneeling before the Apis buried in his sixth year, 524 BCE, with a sarcophagus he gave. Herodotus says he stabbed the Apis in the thigh and went mad. Polyaenus, much later, has Darius offer 100 talents of gold for a new Apis.

Serapeum stelae (Louvre); Herodotus 3.27-29; Polyaenus 7.11.7

Amun at Hibis

در اسناد

In the Kharga oasis, a temple of Amun carries Darius's cartouches and shows him as Pharaoh with Egypt's gods, among the best preserved work of the period. Its dates and how much is Darius's own are argued, but it shows a Persian king paying for an Egyptian god far from any audience.

Temple of Hibis; Cruz-Uribe 1988

Opet, and why it isn't here

در اسناد

Egypt's great feast of Opet belonged to Thebes, where Amun went by boat to Luxor in the second month of the flood. The game has no Thebes, so Memphis keeps its own: Sokar going round the walls, on a date the Medinet Habu calendar gives.

Medinet Habu calendar
