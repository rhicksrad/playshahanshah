Aryandes

Persian satrap of Egypt under Darius, put to death after 518 BCE

Aryandes was the Persian governor of Egypt, appointed by Cambyses according to Herodotus and still in post under Darius. Herodotus tells of the army he sent against Barca in Libya and of the silver coinage that led Darius to have him killed. The story shows how a satrap's position depended on the King's trust.

Satrap of Egypt

Ancient account

Herodotus says Cambyses appointed Aryandes governor of Egypt and that he held the post under Darius. Herodotus gives no account of his earlier career. His rule is known mainly from the episodes Herodotus chose to tell.

Herodotus 4.166

The army against Barca

Ancient account

Herodotus says Aryandes sent an army and fleet against Barca to avenge the killing of Arcesilaus III of Cyrene, whose mother Pheretime had sought his help. Amasis, a Maraphian, commanded the land force and Badres, a Pasargad, the fleet. Barca fell after a long siege, and Herodotus says many of its people were settled in Bactria.

Herodotus 4.167

The siege trick

Later legend

Herodotus tells that the Persians dug tunnels under Barca, and that a smith with a bronze shield detected the digging and the Barcaeans countered it. The Persians then took the city by a sworn truce they never meant to keep. The tale has the shape of a moral story about broken oaths, and it cannot be checked against any Persian record.

Herodotus 4.200

Silver coinage and death

Ancient account

Herodotus says Aryandes struck silver of the purest quality, in rivalry with Darius's gold coinage, which Herodotus calls the purest of all. Darius, he says, then had Aryandes put to death on a charge of rebellion. Herodotus gives the coinage as the reason Darius was angered.

Herodotus 4.166

What historians doubt

Disputed

Modern historians question whether coinage alone could have caused his death and suspect other grounds for the charge. The date of his execution is uncertain, and so is which surviving coins, if any, were his.

P. Briant, From Cyrus to Alexander, 2002
