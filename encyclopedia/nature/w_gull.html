Gulls

Seabirds on the Aegean coasts in the campaigns of 490-479 BCE

In the game: Level 8: The Bay of Marathon, Level 9: The Canal, Level 10: The Hot Gates

Gulls are drawn along the coasts where the Persian fleet and army fought in Greece. No ancient source ties them to these campaigns, so their place in the game comes from the setting, not from a reported fact. The sea shaped the whole war, and the birds mark that setting.

In the game: The game draws gulls as birds overhead on the Athos, Marathon and Thermopylae levels. They are scenery only, and no rule reads them. Their presence is an atmospheric choice, not drawn from a source.

A coast the sources describe without gulls

Ancient account

Herodotus describes the landing at Marathon, the digging of the Athos canal and the fighting at Thermopylae at length. In the passages cited here he speaks of ships, winds and beaches, but gulls do not appear in them. Any gull on these shores is therefore supplied by the modern reader, not by the ancient writer.

Herodotus 6.102

The sea in the campaign narratives

Ancient account

Herodotus says that a storm wrecked much of Mardonius's fleet off Athos in 492. He later says that Xerxes had a canal cut across the peninsula, and gives pride and a wish to show his power as the reason. The narrative shows how much the war depended on the sea, but it does not mention birds.

Herodotus 6.44

Seabirds in Greek writing

Ancient account

Aristotle, writing in the fourth century, discusses sea and shore birds in his works on animals. His texts show that Greeks knew such birds well, but they do not place any gull at a particular battle.

Aristotle, History of Animals

What the game assumes

Disputed

Gulls live along the Aegean today, and nothing in the sources suggests that the coasts of Attica, Athos or the Malian Gulf lacked seabirds in antiquity. This is an inference from the present, not a documented fact for any single species. The game uses gulls as colour only.

Herodotus 7.22-24
