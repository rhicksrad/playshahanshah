Fire, altars and the temple question

What burned, and where

The King stands before a burning altar over his tomb at Naqsh-e Rostam. Two stone plinths stand at Pasargadae. Whether the Achaemenids had fire temples as later Iran did is far less sure, so the game does not build one.

游戏中： The Altar of the Magi and the Fire Altar are open to the sky. Kindling the fire is a rite you can stage at them. Its form is the game's.

In the open air

见于史载

Darius's tomb relief shows him on a stepped base before an altar with a flame. Two limestone plinths stand in the sacred precinct at Pasargadae, one with a stair, and their use is inferred. Mark Garrison's study of the seals shows how varied such images were, and asks how sure even the tomb's fire altar is.

Naqsh-e Rostam, tomb of Darius; Stronach 1978; Garrison 2017

No temples, says Herodotus

古代记述

Herodotus says the Persians build no statues, temples or altars, and think those who do are fools. They climb the mountains to sacrifice. He's too neat: the tablets name altars and offerings, and Darius says he rebuilt sanctuaries. But nothing in his Persia needs a roof over the fire.

Herodotus 1.131-132; Behistun, DB 14

The temple question

争夺中

Later Zoroastrians keep fire in temples, and classical writers describe them, some in Parthian times. Stone buildings such as the Ka'ba-ye Zardosht and the older Nush-i Jan have been called fire temples and denied the name. Encyclopaedia Iranica warns that the words fire altar and fire temple are used loosely. Mary Boyce dated temple fires to the later Achaemenids.

Iranica, "Fire altars"; Boyce 1982

Artaxerxes II's statues

古代记述

Berossus, quoted by Clement of Alexandria, says Artaxerxes II first set up statues of Anahita at Babylon, Susa and Ecbatana. That's after 405 BCE. It is why Anahita has no shrine in this game, whose last level is 479 BCE.

Berossus in Clement, Protrepticus 5.65; A2Sa
